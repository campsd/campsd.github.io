abstract: "The Kibble-Zurek mechanism quantifies defect formation during finite-rate passage across a continuous phase transition, providing key insights into universality in quantum many-body systems. We explore counting statistics of defects in finite-rate ramp experiments on long 1D Rydberg atom chains. The experiments reveal an anomaly in the defect number distribution at long ramp times, challenging the hypothesis of defect formation through independent domain mergers. Numerical simulations reproduce the anomaly and support an interpretation in terms of post-critical coarsening during the ramp, while prepare-and-hold experiments reveal no subsequent secular growth of the measured correlations. Our results show the limits of classical or integrable system descriptions of Kibble-Zurek defect formation for interacting quantum systems."
